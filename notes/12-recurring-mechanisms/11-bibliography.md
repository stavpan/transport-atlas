# Bibliography

> **How to read this list.** Entries are grouped by theme and marked **[P]** primary literature,
> **[R]** review or monograph, **[S]** sceptical/corrective, **[E]** encyclopaedic reference. Volume and
> page numbers are given where they are standard and stable, but **verify the DOI before citing anything
> in your own work**: this is a map of *where to look*, not a citation database. Where a claim in this
> part rests on a single number (Lyapunov times, exponents, dates), that number is marked "(as quoted)"
> and its source is given, so you can disagree with it deliberately rather than accidentally.

## The river and the idea of change

* **[P]** Heraclitus of Ephesus, fragments on the river (Diels–Kranz B12, B91) — "one cannot step twice
  into the same river". The fragments survive only as quotations by later authors, which is why
  interpretations differ so widely; see Plato, *Cratylus* 402a, and Aristotle's reports in the
  *Metaphysics*.
* **[R]** G. S. Kirk, J. E. Raven & M. Schofield, *The Presocratic Philosophers*, 2nd ed. (Cambridge,
  1983) — the scholarly treatment; use it to avoid the romanticised Heraclitus of popular writing.
* **[P]** Plutarch, *Life of Theseus* — the "Ship of Theseus" puzzle of identity through replacement:
  the philosophical twin of the steady-state river in
  [the river as a model](../13-panta-rhei/06-the-river-as-a-model.md).
* **[R]** I. Prigogine, *From Being to Becoming* (1980); I. Prigogine & I. Stengers, *Order out of Chaos*
  (1984) — dissipative structures for a broad audience; read the sweeping claims with the caveats in
  [irreversibility & the arrow of time](../13-panta-rhei/04-irreversibility-and-the-arrow-of-time.md).

## Equilibria, stability, bifurcation

* **[P]** A. M. Lyapunov (1892), *The General Problem of the Stability of Motion* — the second method,
  and the reason "Lyapunov function" is a verb in this literature.
* **[R]** S. H. Strogatz, *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos*, 2nd ed. (2015) — the standard accessible route
  to equilibria, Jacobians, the four portraits, bifurcations and the logistic map.
* **[R]** J. Guckenheimer & P. Holmes, *Nonlinear Oscillations, Dynamical Systems, and Bifurcations of
  Vector Fields* (Springer, 1983) — normal forms, centre manifolds, Hopf theory.
* **[P]** E. Hopf (1942), *Ber. Math.-Phys. Kl. Sächs. Akad. Wiss. Leipzig* 94, 3 — the original Hopf
  bifurcation, giving birth to a limit cycle from a fixed point.
* **[P]** A. M. Turing (1952), "The chemical basis of morphogenesis", *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 237, 37 —
  diffusion-driven instability; the paper that made "which wavelength survives?" a physical question.
* **[P]** R. Thom (1972), *Stabilité structurelle et morphogenèse*; **[R]** E. C. Zeeman (1976),
  "Catastrophe theory", *Scientific American* 234, 65 — source and popularisation.
* **[S]** H. Zahler & H. J. Sussmann (1977), "Claims and accomplishments of applied catastrophe theory",
  *Nature* 269, 759 — the critique that ended the uncritical boom and that later applications still have
  to answer.
* **[R]** M. Scheffer et al. (2009), "Early-warning signals for critical transitions", *Nature* 461, 53 —
  critical slowing down as a measurable leading indicator.

## Criticality, scaling and avalanches

* **[P]** J. Maldacena (1998), *Adv. Theor. Math. Phys.* 2, 231 — the gauge/gravity duality: the most
  ambitious duality in current physics, and a working computational tool for strongly coupled transport.

## How to check a claim in this part

A short protocol, offered because most disagreements in this literature are about method rather than
about physics:

1. **Find the definition.** "Stable", "critical", "chaotic" and "self-organised" all have precise
   meanings and loose uses. Ask which one the claim needs.
2. **Separate a mechanism from a statistic.** A power law, a fat tail or a $1/f$ spectrum is an
   *observation*; the mechanism must be argued separately, and any alternative mechanism that reproduces
   the statistic has to be ruled out.
3. **Look for the collapse.** One dataset with a fitted exponent is weak evidence; a family of datasets
   collapsing under rescaling is strong. Exponents should reproduce across sizes and methods.
4. **Check the separation of scales.** Delay, memory, criticality and homogenisation each require a
   timescale or lengthscale separation. If you cannot state the ratio and check it, the mechanism may
   simply not be operating.
5. **Check the estimator.** Log-binning, subsampling, finite windows and surrogate tests all shift
   reported exponents. Report the method with the number, or the number is not usable.
6. **Prefer an intervention.** The strongest evidence for a mechanism is that changing its strength
   changes the phenomenon as predicted — a knob, not a correlation.
