# Universality: the same mathematics everywhere

This part of the atlas makes the strong claim explicit and then *tests* it, domain by domain:
**almost every model of change we build is a flow of a density — and flows of densities are that one
balance law, its generators, its functionals and its metrics.** The claim is about mathematics, not
about the world: it says that *if* you can name what is being redistributed, the rest of the atlas
applies. What it does not say is written down just as carefully in the last note.

## How to use this part

* If you have a **model** from another field, start at the [lookup table](02-decision-tree-and-lookup.md):
  find your object, read the transport form, follow the link.
* If you want the **argument**, read [why everything flows](01-why-everything-flows.md) — it is a
  theorem-by-theorem statement of what makes a system a transport system.
* If you are **sceptical** (you should be), read the [limits note](09-limits-of-the-claim.md) first. A
  claim that cannot say where it fails is not a claim, it is advertising.

> **The one-sentence version.** Any quantity you can count, whose changes can be attributed to movement
> across boundaries plus creation and destruction, is a density obeying the transport equation; and if
> the movement is driven by a gradient, a potential, an entropy, an optimality principle or a random walk,
> then the whole machinery of the atlas — classification, well-posedness, numerics, limits, universality
> classes — applies unchanged. What is *not* covered is the meaning of the quantity, the measurement of
> it, and the discreteness and semantics of the structures it lives on.
